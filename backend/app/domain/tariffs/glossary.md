- A **rule** is a tariff-code prefix, a rate, an effective date and an origin; an **exclusion** beats the prefixes. The duty follows **origin and code**, never the supplier's country; only the new duty is counted, on the price without freight.
- The duty on a part comes in three buckets, each with its own one-sentence rule: **owed** (certain: confirmed origin, entering on or after the date), **conditional** (open orders that owe nothing if the supplier ships them before the export date and they enter before the end of the window; the ship date is unknown) and **if China** (the part's origin is unknown). Show owed as the figure; show the others beside it, never added in.
- The **importer of record** pays: the company for FOB, FCA, EXW; the supplier for delivered-duty-paid, which is passed through in price. A blank Incoterm is unknown.
- Unknowns it can hold: origin, tariff code, the ship date, the origin a customs broker must rule.
